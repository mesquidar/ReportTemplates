Identity Attack Model

The model follows changes in identity authority: how access is obtained, expanded, transferred and converted into control.

FOOTHOLDInitial identityValid session, endpoint or service context > EXPANDCredential reachSecrets, tickets, tokens and delegated rights > CONTROLPrivileged authorityAdministrative action across a trust boundary
Identity materialPasswords, hashes, tickets, tokens, certificates, keys and recovery secrets.
Control relationshipsGroup membership, delegation, service control, policy rights, ACLs and federation claims.
Administrative surfacesManagement hosts, remote protocols, automation accounts, backup systems and identity consoles.
Evidence requiredA reproducible relationship between a starting principal, an exposed control and a protected authority.

Assessment questions

Can authority cross a boundary?Review paths from standard access to administrative or recovery roles.Can identity material be reused?Review credential placement, session isolation and delegation constraints.
Can control changes be observed?Review telemetry for group, policy, trust and credential events.Can the identity plane be restored?Review protected backups, emergency access and trusted rebuild procedures.

Validation and Recovery Readiness

Identity control work is complete only when the dangerous route is closed, the closure is observable and trusted authority can be restored.

RETESTRoute closedThe original identity relationship no longer yields the verified authority. > OBSERVEControl visibleExpected identity events reach the responsible monitoring function. > RESTOREAuthority recoverableProtected backups and emergency identities support a trusted recovery.

Closure evidence standard

Control claimEvidence expectedAcceptance condition
Privilege route removedConfiguration export, relationship query and failed reproductionThe original principal cannot cross the documented authority boundary.

Detection operatingTest event, alert record and response ownershipThe control change and suspicious reuse are visible within the agreed response window.

Risk interpretation

RatingIdentity interpretationExpected response
CriticalDirect or highly reliable path to protected administrative authority, identity-wide compromise or loss of trusted recovery.Contain immediately and govern as an active identity security risk.
HighPractical path to significant privilege, reusable high-value credentials or broad identity control.Accelerate remediation and validate route closure.
MediumMeaningful control weakness that requires preconditions or contributes to a larger privilege route.Remediate through planned identity hardening.
LowLimited identity exposure or hygiene weakness with constrained authority impact.Address during baseline improvement.
InfoObservation that supports assurance, visibility or architectural understanding.Track as context or control maturity work.

Evidence handling

MinimiseRetain only the identity material required to support a finding. Redact secrets, tokens and personal data wherever possible.
ProtectStore report evidence in an access-controlled location with an owner, retention period and disposal method.
TracePreserve enough context to connect each artefact to the affected identity, control relationship and assessment action.
ValidateRetest using controlled identities and record both the expected denial and the monitoring outcome.

Identity Attack Model

The model follows changes in identity authority: how access is obtained, expanded, transferred and converted into control.

FOOTHOLDInitial identityValid session, endpoint or service context > EXPANDCredential reachSecrets, tickets, tokens and delegated rights > CONTROLPrivileged authorityAdministrative action across a trust boundary
Identity materialPasswords, hashes, tickets, tokens, certificates, keys and recovery secrets.
Control relationshipsGroup membership, delegation, service control, policy rights, ACLs and federation claims.
Administrative surfacesManagement hosts, remote protocols, automation accounts, backup systems and identity consoles.
Evidence requiredA reproducible relationship between a starting principal, an exposed control and a protected authority.

Assessment questions

Can authority cross a boundary?Review paths from standard access to administrative or recovery roles.Can identity material be reused?Review credential placement, session isolation and delegation constraints.
Can control changes be observed?Review telemetry for group, policy, trust and credential events.Can the identity plane be restored?Review protected backups, emergency access and trusted rebuild procedures.
